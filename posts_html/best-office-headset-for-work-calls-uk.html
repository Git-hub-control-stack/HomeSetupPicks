Wired, wireless or ANC: pick the situation, not the spec sheet

The headset aisle is sorted by feature count, which makes the most loaded one look like the right one. It usually is not, because the real decision is where and how you work, not which list is longest. Stay put at one desk all day and a wired USB headset is the path of least resistance: live the second the computer wakes, no link to drop and no charge to run down mid-sentence. That is the whole case for the Logitech H390. Get up on calls, pace while you think, or want a laptop and a phone reachable at once, and a wireless headset with multipoint earns its place, which is where the Jabra Evolve2 65 comes in. Active noise cancelling is a separate axis rather than a rung above the others: it counts only if the room around you has a steady background to quieten, and for a shared or open-plan home that is what the Poly Voyager Focus 2 is for. Decide which of those three fits your day, and you have your headset.

The upgrade the other end hears is the microphone

It is easy to spend on the earcups and forget that, on a call, the part that changes other people's experience is the microphone. A laptop mic sits half a desk away and hauls in whatever else the room is doing, from the cooling fan to your own typing; a headset parks a dedicated mic a few centimetres from your lips, so your voice lands in front of that din instead of under it. A boom that reaches towards the mouth, like the one on the H390 and the Voyager Focus 2, gets closest of all, and it is the single step the far end notices most. The Evolve2 65 keeps its mics in the earcup rather than on a boom, which looks tidier and still beats a laptop, just by a smaller margin. If being understood first time matters on your calls, weigh the mic before anything else on the sheet.

What active noise cancelling does, and what it doesn't

Active noise cancelling is a headline feature on the spec sheet, so it helps to be clear about its job. It uses the headset's own microphones to counter steady, droning sound around you, a fan or the hum of an air-conditioning unit, so the wearer hears less of it. There are two things it does not do. It does not change how you sound to the people you are calling; that is the mic's job. And it does little against sudden noises like a slammed door or a barking dog. At a quiet home desk the padded cushions on any of these headsets already block a fair amount, so ANC is a feature you can skip; in a shared room or an open-plan space with a constant background it is worth having, which is why it sits on the Voyager Focus 2 as a switchable, three-level setting you can turn down or off.

Multipoint, certification and all-day comfort

Three smaller specs settle the rest. Multipoint lets a headset hold a connection to two devices at once, so a call can arrive on your phone while the headset is paired to the laptop, with no re-pairing in between; the Evolve2 65 has it, and it genuinely helps if you take mobile calls at your desk. Certification, the Microsoft Teams badge on the Evolve2 65 and the Teams and Google Meet badges on the Voyager Focus 2, means the physical answer, end and mute keys drive the named app rather than lighting an indicator of their own; lacking it, the audio is no different; the call controls simply live on screen instead of under your thumb. Comfort is the one that is easy to underrate on something you wear for hours: an over-ear like the Voyager seals around the ear and isolates more but runs warmer, while an on-ear like the Evolve2 65 is lighter and cooler but rests its weight on the ear. There is no single right answer there, only the fit that suits your ears and your day.

FAQ

Q: Do I need a headset, or will my laptop or a speakerphone do? A: For the odd quick call a laptop is fine. For back-to-back calls a headset keeps the conversation to you, puts a proper microphone near your mouth, and in a shared home stops your calls filling the room. A speakerphone is the hands-free route, best in a room of your own where wearing something all day feels like too much; a fair number run both and pick by how the day's calls look.

Q: Wired or wireless for a home desk? A: A desk you sit at all day rarely needs wireless. Plug a wired headset in and it is simply always there, nothing to pair, nothing to recharge, no link to lose, which makes it the low-effort answer for a spot you never leave. Go wireless the moment you actually move on calls, or want your phone paired alongside the laptop; that is the line between the wired H390 and the cordless Evolve2 65.

Q: Does active noise cancelling make my calls clearer? A: Not to the person you are calling. ANC takes the edge off a constant drone for you, the wearer, so you hear less of the room while you talk; how you come across at the other end is the mic's doing entirely. If your calls are hard to follow, look at mic quality and placement first.

Q: What is multipoint, and do I need it? A: It is the trick that keeps a wireless headset talking to two devices together, typically a work laptop and your own phone, so an incoming mobile call lands without you unpairing and pairing again. If you take calls on both at your desk it is a real convenience; if everything runs through the laptop you will not miss it. Of these three, the Evolve2 65 is the one that offers it.

Q: Will any of these just work with Teams, Zoom and Meet? A: Yes. Any meeting app treats a headset as a plain audio input, picked up over its USB lead or Bluetooth link the moment you plug in or pair, with nothing to install. What certification changes is the buttons: a Teams or Meet badge wires the headset's answer, end and mute keys into that one app. The Evolve2 65 holds the badge for Microsoft Teams, the Voyager Focus 2 for Teams and Google Meet. Skip the badge and the call sounds identical; you simply mute from the meeting window rather than from the earcup.
