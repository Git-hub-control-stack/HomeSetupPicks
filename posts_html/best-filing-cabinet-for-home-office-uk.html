Paper has not gone away. Contracts, warranties, tax records and the things you are told to "keep the original of" still need a drawer that locks, and a home office rarely has the floor space an office does. So the filing cabinet you want is not the biggest one: it is the one that holds your actual filing, locks, and fits the gap you have, whether that gap is the slot under a desk or a corner of the room. The facts that settle it are the drawer count, whether the drawers take A4 or the deeper Foolscap suspension files, whether it locks, whether it rolls or stays put, and its footprint against the space you are giving it. This guide scores three UK picks on what their listings actually state, and deliberately spans three shapes rather than lining up three near-identical cabinets, because the right one depends far more on where it goes than on brand.

What decides a home-office filing cabinet

A filing cabinet is bought on a handful of facts, and the one most buyers get wrong is leading with drawer count instead of fit.

  1. Where it has to go, and how tall that lets it be. The first measurement is not the cabinet, it is your gap. A mobile cabinet or pedestal under about 60 cm tall can live under a desk; a full-height unit over a metre stands beside one and needs clear floor. Measure the slot under your desk apron before you choose a shape, and if you are also sizing the desk itself our desk-depth guide covers the clearance a pedestal needs underneath.
  2. A4 or Foolscap, and suspension files. UK filing hangs in suspension files, and those come in A4 and the slightly wider Foolscap. A drawer built only for A4 will not take a Foolscap file, so if your existing files are Foolscap, check the drawer says so, as the pedestal and the full-height pick here both state.
  3. The lock, and how many keys. The reason to buy a cabinet rather than a drawer unit is that it locks. Check it comes with a key and whether one key covers every drawer, since a linked lock is one turn to secure the lot. All three of our picks state a lock, and two tell you how many keys come with it; always confirm it on the live listing.
  4. Mobile or fixed, and anti-tilt. Casters let a cabinet roll out for the back drawer and away afterwards, which is worth a lot in a small room. A tall fixed cabinet should instead have an anti-tilt interlock so only one loaded drawer opens at once, because a full upper drawer on an empty base is how cabinets tip. If you are tidying the rest of the desk while you are at it, our desk mat and cable management guide and the under-desk keyboard tray guide cover the gear that shares that footprint.

Get those four right and the drawer count takes care of itself: buy the shape that fits your space and your file size, then the one with enough drawers for what you actually keep.

FAQ

Q: What is the difference between A4 and Foolscap filing? A: Both are suspension-file sizes that hang on rails inside a drawer, but Foolscap is a little wider than A4, so a Foolscap drawer takes A4 files too while an A4-only drawer will not take Foolscap. If your existing files are the wider Foolscap, buy a cabinet that states Foolscap, like the pedestal or the full-height pick here.

Q: Do these filing cabinets lock? A: Yes, all three state a lock. The compact overall pick closes all three drawers with the same key, and the full-height cabinet comes with two keys; the pedestal locks as well. A lock is the main reason to choose a filing cabinet over an open drawer unit, so confirm it is listed before you buy.

Q: Which filing cabinet fits under a desk? A: The two mobile picks are the under-desk ones. The compact overall cabinet is 59.2 cm tall and the pedestal 59.5 cm, both low enough for a typical desk, while the full-height steel cabinet is a floor-standing unit that needs its own space instead. Measure the gap under your desk apron first.

Q: Is a mobile cabinet or a full-height one better for a home office? A: For most home offices a mobile cabinet or pedestal wins, because it tucks away and rolls out only when needed. A full-height cabinet is the better buy once you are storing years of records, since it gives three deep drawers and the stability a full archive needs, at the cost of a fixed, floor-standing footprint.

Q: Do they come assembled? A: It varies, so check each listing. The full-height steel cabinet states that it ships assembled, which is worth having on a unit that size; the mobile picks are lighter and more typical to set up at home. The product page is where the assembly note is stated.

Q: Can a filing cabinet double as something else? A: The compact overall pick is listed as a rolling printer stand as well as a file cabinet, so its top can hold a printer while the drawers file beneath it, which suits a small room. The pedestal's oak-effect top sits at desk-side height and takes a lamp or a plant without looking like office furniture.
