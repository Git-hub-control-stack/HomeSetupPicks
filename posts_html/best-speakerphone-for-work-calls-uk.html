Wired or Bluetooth? The choice that settles most of it

Before the microphone count or the brand, settle one thing: do you want a cable. At a desk you sit at every day, a wired USB speakerphone is usually the easier life. It is ready the instant your machine wakes, it never drops a connection halfway through a call, and it never picks the worst possible moment to run its battery down. The Speak2 40 and the PowerConf S330 are both wired for exactly that reason. Bluetooth earns its keep when you move: if your calls happen at the desk on Monday and the kitchen table on Tuesday, or you simply want the desk clear of one more lead, a wireless unit like the Speak2 55 is worth the habit of charging it. If you are honestly at the same desk all week, going cable-free buys a freedom you will never actually use.

The microphone count is really a room measurement

Speakerphone listings love a big microphone number, because more sounds like better. What those arrays are really measuring is how large a table they can cover. A six or eight-microphone ring exists to hear everyone seated around a boardroom; on your own, forty to sixty centimetres from the unit, a focused four-microphone speakerphone hears you clearly and wastes nothing reaching for the far end of a room that is not there. Beamforming, which the Jabras use, goes a step further and points the pickup at where you sit rather than listening everywhere at once, so less of the room behind you rides along on the call. At a solo desk, tighter beats bigger.

Full-duplex and certification: the two specs worth checking

Two things on the spec sheet actually change your calls, and neither is the one printed largest. The first is full-duplex audio. It means both people can talk at the same time and the speakerphone carries both voices instead of ducking one to let the other through. That is the difference between talking and taking turns, and it is where a basic single-microphone puck tends to fall down. The second is platform certification. When a unit is certified for Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams, its physical mute and answer buttons are tied into the app, so pressing mute on the hardware actually mutes you in the meeting. On an uncertified unit that button might mute the hardware without telling the app, so you sit there muted while the meeting waits, or worse, believe you have muted yourself when the room can still hear every word. Work out which platform you live in and match it: the Speak2 40 is certified for Zoom and Google Meet, the Speak2 55 for Microsoft Teams.

FAQ

Q: Do I really need a speakerphone instead of my laptop's built-in mic and speakers? A: For the odd quick call, no. But a laptop microphone sits far from your mouth and picks up the fan and the keyboard, and its speakers echo back into that same microphone, which is what makes people ask you to repeat yourself. A speakerphone puts a proper microphone array near you and tunes the speaker so it does not feed back, so you sound clearer and calls feel less like hard work.

Q: Is a speakerphone or a headset better for work calls? A: It depends on where you sit. A speakerphone is hands-free and natural, with nothing to wear over a long day, which suits a private room. A headset or a pair of earbuds keeps the call to you and sounds cleaner to the other side in a noisy or shared space. Plenty of people keep both and choose by the day.

Q: How many microphones do I actually need? A: At a solo desk, four is plenty. Large microphone counts are built to cover a table of people in a meeting room; on your own, an arm's length from the unit, a focused four-microphone speakerphone hears you clearly and drags less of the room into the call.

Q: Which meeting apps do these speakerphones support? A: To a meeting app each one is just an audio device on USB or Bluetooth, so Teams, Zoom and Meet find it with no driver or download. Where they differ is certification: the Speak2 40 is certified for Zoom and Google Meet and the Speak2 55 for Microsoft Teams, which ties the on-unit mute and call buttons into that app. Without certification a unit still carries your voice perfectly well; you simply reach for the app's own mute rather than the button on top.

Q: Do I need Bluetooth, or is a wired speakerphone enough? A: If you call from the same desk every day, wired is enough and arguably the easier life - there is no pairing step, no battery to watch, and no connection to lose mid-call. Bluetooth is worth it if you move your calls between rooms or you want one less cable on the desk. That is the whole case for the Speak2 55 over the Speak2 40.
