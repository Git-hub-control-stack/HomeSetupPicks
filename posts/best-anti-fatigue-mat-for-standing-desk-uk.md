## The decision most guides skip: flat or contoured

Start here, before any product. A flat mat is the sensible all-rounder for someone at a fixed desk who naturally shifts their weight and swaps between sitting and standing through the day: it is a plain resilient surface, it stays out of the way, and you stop noticing it. A contoured or terrain mat is a different animal, with raised rails and mounds you press into and step around, and it suits a person who likes to keep actively changing stance while they are up. The catch is that the same raised surface some people love is a surface others find they keep noticing when they are trying to concentrate. Neither wins outright. Read yourself honestly: "I just want somewhere better than the floor" points to flat, and "I get restless and like to move about" points to terrain.

## Foam, rubber and how long a mat keeps its cushion

Here is the part the listings gloss over. Most cheap standing mats are foam, either PU or EVA, and foam is light, inexpensive and soft on the first day, but it takes a compression set: stand on the same patches for months and they pack down and stop springing back. Solid rubber and denser polyurethane behave differently, recovering their shape after you step off and holding their cushion for years rather than months. So "what is it made of" is really the question "how long will this stay any good", and it is worth asking before you buy. Two of the picks here, the Duronic and the Sky Solutions, are foam: kind to stand on now, likely to soften with time. The Ergodriven uses a tougher polyurethane terrain skin built to keep its shape. We should be straight that we have not stood on any of these for years on end, so this is how these materials tend to behave, not a stopwatch test of these exact mats.

## Thickness, size and where it sits

Thickness gets quoted like a headline number, and on its own it is close to meaningless. A thick slab of cheap foam that crushes flat under your weight supports you less than a thinner mat in a resilient material that holds its shape, so read thickness and material together, never thickness alone. A decent material at a sensible thickness is the useful combination for standing; too thin and there is little to it, and a lot of soft foam just means you are standing in a dent. Size matters for a different reason: a mat you can nudge aside with one foot when you sit back down, and step straight onto when you stand, is a mat that gets used, whereas one that pins you to a single spot defeats the point of a height-adjustable desk. Measure the patch of floor where you actually stand before you buy, and check each listing's dimensions against it, because standing mats come in quite different footprints and the one that fits your space beats the one with the best write-up.

## The bits that decide whether it survives under a desk

Two unglamorous details separate a mat you keep from one you kick into a corner. The first is the base. On laminate, wood or tile a mat will slide unless the underside grips or the mat has enough weight to stay put, so if you are not on carpet, a non-slip base is not optional. The second is the edges. A mat with bevelled, tapered edges lets a chair castor roll over it and your own toe clear it, while a thick square lip is the thing you catch your foot on or that curls up and trips you. Neither point shows up in a glamour shot, and both are worth checking on the listing photos before you commit.

## FAQ

**Q: Flat or contoured, which mat should I get for a standing desk?**
A: Go flat if you are at a fixed desk and just want a better surface than bare floor to stand and shift about on; it is the quieter, more forgettable choice. Go contoured or terrain if you like to actively change your stance while you are up and do not mind a surface with shape to it. The terrain that some people enjoy is the same terrain others find they keep noticing during focused work, so it comes down to how you like to stand.

**Q: Does a thicker mat mean a better one?**
A: Not by itself. A thick cheap foam that crushes flat holds you up less than a thinner mat in a resilient material, so thickness only means something read next to what the mat is made of. A decent material at a sensible thickness is the useful combination; a lot of soft foam just means you are mostly standing in a dip.

**Q: What are standing mats made of, and why does it matter?**
A: Most are foam (PU or EVA), which is soft and cheap but packs down over months of standing. Solid rubber and denser polyurethane cost more and feel firmer, but they recover their shape and keep their cushion for far longer. The material is the best clue you have to how long a mat will stay worth standing on.

**Q: Will a standing mat slip about on a hard floor?**
A: On a hard floor such as laminate, wood or tile it can, especially if the underside is smooth and there is little weight holding it down. Look for a base that is textured or grippy, or a mat with enough heft to stay tight to the floor. Carpet rarely gives this trouble. If your floor is hard, sort this out before anything else, because a mat that skates out from under you is one you soon stop reaching for.

**Q: What size standing mat do I need?**
A: Big enough to stand on comfortably and to shift your feet around, but small enough to nudge aside with a foot when you sit back down. Measure the floor where you stand at the desk and compare it with each listing's dimensions, because footprints vary a lot and the right size for your space matters more than any single feature.
