## What decides a home-office filing cabinet

A filing cabinet is bought on a handful of facts, and the one most buyers get wrong is leading with drawer count instead of fit.

1. **Where it has to go, and how tall that lets it be.** The first measurement is not the cabinet, it is your gap. A mobile cabinet or pedestal under about 60 cm tall can live under a desk; a full-height unit over a metre stands beside one and needs clear floor. Measure the slot under your desk apron before you choose a shape, and if you are also sizing the desk itself our [desk-depth guide](how-deep-should-a-home-office-desk-be.html) covers the clearance a pedestal needs underneath.
2. **A4 or Foolscap, and suspension files.** UK filing hangs in suspension files, and those come in A4 and the slightly wider Foolscap. A drawer built only for A4 will not take a Foolscap file, so if your existing files are Foolscap, check the drawer says so, as the pedestal and the full-height pick here both state.
3. **The lock, and how many keys.** The reason to buy a cabinet rather than a drawer unit is that it locks. Check it comes with a key and whether one key covers every drawer, since a linked lock is one turn to secure the lot. All three of our picks state a lock, and two tell you how many keys come with it; always confirm it on the live listing.
4. **Mobile or fixed, and anti-tilt.** Casters let a cabinet roll out for the back drawer and away afterwards, which is worth a lot in a small room. A tall fixed cabinet should instead have an anti-tilt interlock so only one loaded drawer opens at once, because a full upper drawer on an empty base is how cabinets tip. If you are tidying the rest of the desk while you are at it, our [desk mat and cable management guide](best-desk-mat-and-cable-management-uk.html) and the [under-desk keyboard tray guide](best-under-desk-keyboard-tray-uk.html) cover the gear that shares that footprint.

Get those four right and the drawer count takes care of itself: buy the shape that fits your space and your file size, then the one with enough drawers for what you actually keep.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between A4 and Foolscap filing?**
A: Both are suspension-file sizes that hang on rails inside a drawer, but Foolscap is a little wider than A4, so a Foolscap drawer takes A4 files too while an A4-only drawer will not take Foolscap. If your existing files are the wider Foolscap, buy a cabinet that states Foolscap, like the pedestal or the full-height pick here.

**Q: Do these filing cabinets lock?**
A: Yes, all three state a lock. The compact overall pick closes all three drawers with the same key, and the full-height cabinet comes with two keys; the pedestal locks as well. A lock is the main reason to choose a filing cabinet over an open drawer unit, so confirm it is listed before you buy.

**Q: Which filing cabinet fits under a desk?**
A: The two mobile picks are the under-desk ones. The compact overall cabinet is 59.2 cm tall and the pedestal 59.5 cm, both low enough for a typical desk, while the full-height steel cabinet is a floor-standing unit that needs its own space instead. Measure the gap under your desk apron first.

**Q: Is a mobile cabinet or a full-height one better for a home office?**
A: For most home offices a mobile cabinet or pedestal wins, because it tucks away and rolls out only when needed. A full-height cabinet is the better buy once you are storing years of records, since it gives three deep drawers and the stability a full archive needs, at the cost of a fixed, floor-standing footprint.

**Q: Do they come assembled?**
A: It varies, so check each listing. The full-height steel cabinet states that it ships assembled, which is worth having on a unit that size; the mobile picks are lighter and more typical to set up at home. The product page is where the assembly note is stated.

**Q: Can a filing cabinet double as something else?**

The pedestal's oak-effect top sits at desk-side height and takes a lamp or a plant without looking like office furniture.
