## What a work call actually needs from a camera

1. **Resolution is the spec that sells cameras and the one a call throws away.** A meeting app compresses and downscales your video on the way out, so a 2K or higher sensor spends most of its detail before the call ever sends it. A clean 1080p image, which every pick here manages, is as much as the other side is going to see. Put the money on the lens, the focus and the light instead.
2. **Field of view decides how much of your room turns up.** The C920S frames at 78 degrees, which holds one person without much of the wall behind them; the Brio 100 is tighter still at 58 degrees. Ultra-wide cameras that boast a big view are built for a group around a table, and on a solo desk they mostly invite the clutter behind you into the shot. Narrower is usually the friend of a tidy call.
3. **Focus and light do more for how you look than megapixels.** Autofocus keeps you sharp as you move, which is the C920S's edge over the fixed-focus Brio 100. Auto light correction, which the Brio 100 has, lifts a dim room so you are lit from the front rather than left as a shadow against a window. ## FAQ

**Q: Is a higher-resolution webcam worth it for calls?**
A: For a call, not really. Meeting apps compress and shrink your video, so most of the detail from a 2K or higher sensor never reaches the other person. A solid 1080p camera, like all three here, sends everything the call can carry, so the money is better spent on focus, framing and lighting.

**Q: Is a webcam actually better than my laptop's built-in camera for work calls?**
A: Almost always. A laptop camera is a tiny sensor squeezed into a thin lid and angled up at your chin. A separate webcam sits at eye level on top of your screen, uses a bigger lens, and focuses and lights better. It is one of the easiest upgrades to how you come across on a call.

**Q: Does a webcam's built-in mic sound good enough?**
A: For short calls, yes. For anything longer, a headset or a dedicated microphone will sound clearer to the people listening, because a webcam's mics sit an arm's length from your mouth. Treat the camera as the picture and the audio as a separate decision.

**Q: Do I need a privacy shutter?**
A: It is reassuring rather than essential. A physical shutter, which the C920S and Brio 100 both have, blocks the lens itself, which many people trust more than a software "camera off". The Anker C200 leaves it out, so if that matters to you, factor in a stick-on cover.

**Q: Will these work with Teams, Zoom and Google Meet?**
A: Yes. All three connect over standard USB, and the main meeting apps let you choose your webcam in their camera settings on Windows or Mac. Webcams like these are plug and play, so there is nothing to install before your first call.
