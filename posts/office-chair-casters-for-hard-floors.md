# Which Office Chair Casters Are Safe For Hard Floors

## Why the wheels your chair came with are usually the wrong ones

Most task chairs leave the factory on hard plastic casters, and those are built for carpet. Herman Miller, which sells replacement casters for its own chairs, describes its standard carpet caster plainly: it is made of plastic and is "perfect for use on low- to medium-pile carpet." On carpet that hard wheel works well, rolling over the pile instead of sinking into it. Move the same chair onto a wood, laminate or tile floor and the problem shows up two ways: a hard wheel has less to grip with, so the chair skids when you push off, and the same hard edge can mark the surface it is rolling on. The wheel that felt fine on the bedroom carpet is the wrong tool the moment the floor turns hard.

## What a hard-floor caster actually is

A hard-floor caster swaps the hard plastic for a soft tread, usually polyurethane or rubber, and often wraps it around a larger twin wheel, which is the "rollerblade" style you will see advertised. The soft tread is the whole point. Herman Miller says of its polyurethane caster that "thanks to its polyurethane tread, it's also safe for hard floors." Steelcase, describing its own soft casters, is blunter about the job they do: they are "perfect for wood, laminate, tile, and other hard surfaces," with a "scratch-resistant design to protect your flooring." The softer tread grips the floor, so the chair does not shoot out from under you, and it rolls over the surface rather than scoring a line across it. That is the trade a hard-floor caster makes: a little more grip and a lot less risk to the floor.

## How to tell which ones you have

You do not need the box or the model number to find out which casters are on your chair. Tip the chair back and look at a wheel. A hard, usually black plastic wheel with no separate tread is a carpet caster. A wheel with a softer band around its edge, often grey or a translucent off-white, is the hard-floor type. Most chairs ship with the carpet kind unless the listing says otherwise, so if you cannot remember choosing soft casters, you almost certainly have the hard ones.

## Swapping them, and the other way round the problem

On most task chairs the casters are not fixed in place: each wheel has a stem that pushes into a socket under the base, and it pulls straight out by hand and pushes back in the same way, with no tools. The one thing worth checking first is that the replacement matches your chair's own fitting, because not every base uses the same stem, so pull one of your existing casters out and take it with you rather than guessing. If you would rather not change the wheels at all, a chair mat is the other route: a hard-floor mat sits under the chair and takes the wear instead of the floor, though it adds a surface to the room and can slide on a very smooth floor. Either way, this is a fix to the chair you already own. ## FAQ

**Q: Will an office chair really mark a wooden or laminate floor?**
A: A hard carpet caster can skid on a hard floor and can mark it over time, which is exactly why the makers sell a different wheel for the job. Steelcase describes its soft casters as "scratch-resistant" and designed "to protect your flooring," and that is the clearest sign that the standard hard wheel is not meant for a hard surface. It is not a guarantee in either direction, since grit caught under a wheel and how hard you push both play a part, but the soft tread is the side of the bet the makers themselves land on.

**Q: Will "rollerblade" soft casters fit my chair?**
A: Usually, because most office chairs use a push-in stem and soft casters are sold to match the common sizes, but "usually" is not "always." The safe move is to pull one caster out of your chair and check the stem against the replacement's before you order, rather than trust a listing that says it fits every chair. If the chair itself is the weak link, a better base is worth more than better wheels, and our [office chair guide](best-office-chair-under-300-uk.html) covers that.

**Q: Is a chair mat as good as swapping to soft casters?**
A: Both protect the floor, and which is better depends on what you mind. A mat needs no change to the chair, but it puts a plastic or glass surface on your floor that you then have to look at and keep from sliding. Soft casters leave the floor clear and roll on it directly, at the cost of a few minutes swapping wheels. For a floor you care about the look of, the casters are the tidier answer.
