# Best Dual Monitor Arm UK 2026

Two monitors change the maths. A single arm only has to balance one screen over one clamp; a dual mount asks one desk edge to carry both, keeps two heads level with each other, and still leaves enough reach for the second screen to swing where you want it. That is why a dual arm is not simply two single arms bolted together. The numbers that decide it are the weight each arm is rated to hold, whether the two screens hang off one shared pole or each rides its own independent arm, and the VESA pattern and desk edge they both have to share. This guide leads with those, and scores three UK-available dual arms on what their own listings actually publish, not on how many screens the product photo happens to show.

## What a second screen adds to the maths

A dual arm is bought on the same ideas as a single arm, with one extra load on each of them.

1. **Weight, per arm and combined.** Check each monitor's panel weight (in its spec sheet, stand excluded) against the arm's per-arm rating, and leave headroom. Then remember the clamp holds both at once: the desk edge feels the combined weight even though each arm is rated on its own. Our single-screen [monitor arm guide](best-monitor-arm-uk.html) covers the one-screen version of this if a dual setup turns out to be more than you need.
2. **Independent arms, not a fixed crossbar.** Every pick here gives each screen its own articulating arm on a shared pole, so you can angle the pair toward you in a shallow V, or swing one aside for a meeting and leave the other square on. A cheaper rigid crossbar holds two screens in one flat line instead, with far less give. If you want to position the two separately, that independence is the feature worth paying for.
3. **VESA and the desk edge they share.** Both screens need the four-hole VESA square the arm takes (75x75 or 100x100 here), and the clamp needs a few centimetres of solid, accessible edge to grip. Our [VESA compatibility guide](monitor-arm-vesa-compatibility-explained.html) walks through finding the pattern and the no-holes case, and the [desk-fit guide](monitor-arm-desk-fit-explained.html) measures the edge and the grommet route. If one screen stays put at a height that already suits you, a [riser rather than an arm](monitor-riser-vs-monitor-arm.html) may be the simpler call for it.

Getting the two heights right is the same job as on a single screen: bring the top of each panel level with your eyes. Our [monitor height guide](monitor-height-explained.html) walks through the HSE rule, and a dual arm makes matching the two easy because you set each one by hand.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I mount two different-sized monitors on one arm?**
A: Usually yes, as long as each screen sits inside the arm's stated size range and under its per-arm weight rating on its own. A matched pair is routine on all three picks; mixing a small panel with a much larger one only works on the arm whose range reaches the bigger screen, which here is the overall BONTEC with its wider span.

**Q: Do both my monitors need the same VESA pattern?**
A: Each monitor just needs one of the patterns the arm supports (75x75 or 100x100 on these picks); the two screens do not have to match each other. Check the back of each monitor for the four-hole square, or search its model number plus "VESA". A screen with no holes needs its maker's adapter plate first.

**Q: Does a two-screen setup need extra cable management?**
A: A second screen adds another set of power and video cables across the desk, so a mount that routes them down the pole or crossbar keeps the clean-desk win. Our [desk mat and cable management guide](best-desk-mat-and-cable-management-uk.html) covers the rest of the run.

**Q: Will a dual arm fit my desk?**
A: A dual clamp grips the same kind of edge a single arm needs, but it carries both screens together, so a flimsy or very thin top feels that combined load. Glass tops, very thick edges and spots over a support beam are the usual blockers; a grommet mount through a cable hole is the fallback where the listing includes one. Our [desk-fit guide](monitor-arm-desk-fit-explained.html) measures it properly.

**Q: What if one of my monitors is heavier than the other?**
A: That is fine as long as the heavier screen is under the per-arm rating on its own, because each arm carries its own screen rather than sharing a load. Set the tension on each arm to its screen; a lighter panel on the other side simply needs less.

**Q: Should I get a dual arm or two single arms?**
A: A dual arm on one pole keeps a tidy centre and takes one clamp point; two single arms give each screen its own base and a wider total spread, but use two clamp points and cost more. For a standard side-by-side pair, one dual mount is the simpler and cheaper route. Our [single monitor arm guide](best-monitor-arm-uk.html) covers the one-screen case.
