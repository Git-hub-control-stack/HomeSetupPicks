# Best Footrest for Under Your Desk UK 2026

Set your chair so the desk lands at the right height for your arms, and there is a good chance your feet stop reaching the floor. That is not a fault in you or the chair; it is arithmetic, and it is the whole reason an under-desk footrest exists. What trips people up is that the word covers three quite different objects: a hard platform you set to a height, a soft cushion you rest on, and a curved board that lets your feet move. They suit different people, and picking the wrong type is how a footrest ends up shoved in a cupboard. ## First, check the chair is the reason

Before you buy anything, work out why your feet are not reaching the floor, because half the time the footrest is fixing a chair that is set wrong. When you raise the seat so your forearms sit level with the desk, you often lift your feet clear of the floor at the same time; that is the classic home-office mismatch, and a footrest fills the gap the seat height opened up. So try the chair first. If it drops low enough for your feet to sit flat while your arms still reach the desk, you may not need a footrest at all. If it does not, or if lowering it leaves you reaching up to type, then your feet need their own support and a footrest is the right call. Our [office chair guide](best-office-chair-under-300-uk.html) works through setting the seat height, and it is worth reading first so you buy a footrest because you need one, not because the chair was never set up.

## Platform, cushion or rocker

The three types are not better and worse versions of one thing; they do different jobs, so match the type to your reason for buying. A platform is the pick when the problem is height: it is firm, it sets to a level, and it holds your feet where the floor should be. A cushion is the pick when your feet more or less reach already and you just want something soft to rest them on, with a bit of tilt to angle them. A rocker is the pick when sitting still is the thing you cannot do, or when you want something to shift your feet on at a standing desk. Read your own complaint back before choosing: "my feet dangle" points to the platform, "I want somewhere soft" points to the cushion, and "I fidget" points to the rocker. If you use a [standing desk](best-standing-desk-small-home-office-uk.html) as well as sitting, the rocker is the one that carries over to time on your feet.

## What to check before you buy

Once you have the type, three practical things decide whether it stays put and gets used. The first is the base: on a hard floor a footrest slides unless it has a non-slip underside or enough weight to stay put, so if you are on laminate or wood rather than carpet, check for a grippy base. The second is size and where it sits: measure the gap under your desk, because a broad platform or a wide board needs room to sit without fouling the desk legs or a drawer, and a rest you keep kicking out of the way is a rest you stop using. The third applies mainly to the cushion: a foam top picks up dust and marks, so a removable, washable cover is worth having, while a hard platform or a wooden board just wipes clean. If the rest doubles as a footstool, as the wooden one does, it is also worth a look at how much weight the maker says it takes before you stand on it.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I actually need a footrest under my desk?**
A: Only if your feet still dangle once the chair is set so your forearms are level with the desk. So sort the chair before buying anything: get it low enough for flat feet while your arms still reach, and you need nothing more. If you cannot get both at once, that gap is what a footrest fills.

**Q: What type of footrest is best for a home office?**
A: It depends on your reason for wanting one. Feet that dangle want a firm platform that adjusts to a height. When they roughly reach already and you are just after something soft, a memory-foam cushion suits better. For anyone who cannot sit still, or who works at a standing desk, a rocking board keeps the feet moving. Match the type to the complaint rather than buying on looks.

**Q: Will a footrest slide around on a hard floor?**
A: It can, if it has a smooth base and little weight. On laminate, wood or tile, look for a non-slip underside or a rest with enough weight to stay put. On carpet it is much less of an issue. It is the first thing to check if you are not sitting on carpet, because a rest that slides is one you stop using.

**Q: Should the footrest be flat or angled?**
A: Either can work, and some let you choose. A flat platform gives your feet a level place to sit; an angled or tilting top lets you point your toes up or down to suit how you like them. The foam cushion here lets you set the angle, while the wooden rocker moves through a range as it tips. Pick whichever feels natural rather than chasing a single correct angle.

**Q: Can a footrest double as a footstool?**
A: A solid one can. The wooden rocker here is solid natural wood, so it works as a plain footstool away from the desk as well as a rocker under it. A foam cushion is made to rest feet on rather than take standing weight, so check the maker's stated weight limit before you stand on any footrest.
