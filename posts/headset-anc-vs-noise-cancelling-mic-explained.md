# Noise Cancelling Headsets: ANC Or A Noise-Cancelling Mic

## One phrase, two different parts of the headset

"Noise cancelling" gets printed on two parts of a headset that have nothing to do with each other, and the box almost never says which one it means. One version lives on the speakers and decides how much of the room you hear. The other lives on the microphone and decides how much of the room your caller hears. They pull in opposite directions, they are built and priced separately, and a headset can arrive with one, both or neither. So the useful question is never whether a headset is "noise cancelling." It is which end the cancelling happens at, and whether that is the end your calls actually need.

## ANC works on what reaches your ears

Active noise cancellation, usually shortened to ANC, is the speaker-side feature. The headset listens to the constant low background around you with its own microphones, a desk fan or the drone of a fridge, and plays an opposing sound back so less of it reaches you. Two limits are worth knowing before you pay for it. It does nothing to how you sound at the far end, since that is the microphone's department entirely. And it works on steady hums, not on sharp one-off sounds like a door going or a dog starting up. There is also a quieter version you get for nothing: the ear cushions seal against your head and already block a fair slice of the room with no electronics involved. That is passive isolation, and on a reasonably quiet home desk it covers enough that true ANC is a feature you can skip.

## A noise-cancelling microphone works on what you send

Turn the same phrase around and it describes the opposite job. A noise-cancelling microphone cleans up the sound leaving your headset, so your voice reaches the call in front of the room rather than buried under it. A boom that reaches down towards your mouth is the plainest form, because it simply sits closer to your voice than to anything else in the room. More elaborate sets run several microphones at once and aim the pickup at your voice while holding the background behind it, which is what a name like Acoustic Fence is describing. If the complaint on your calls is that people keep hearing your kitchen or your keyboard, this is the side that answers it, and ANC will do nothing for it.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need active noise cancelling for work calls?**
A: Usually not, if your desk is reasonably quiet. ANC changes what you hear, not what the other side hears, so it does nothing for how clear you sound on a call. The ear cushions already block a fair amount on their own, and the microphone is what decides how clearly you come across. Keep ANC for a genuinely noisy or shared room, where you would rather hear less of a constant background while you talk.

**Q: Will noise cancelling stop my caller hearing my room?**
A: Only the microphone side does that. If people on your calls keep picking up your background, the fix is a noise-cancelling microphone: a boom that sits near your mouth, or a multi-microphone array that aims at your voice. An ANC badge on the audio side will not help, because it works in the wrong direction entirely.

**Q: A headset says "noise cancelling" but only once. Which kind is it?**
A: Look at where the claim sits on the spec sheet. Under the microphone, or beside a boom or a mic-array name, it is the caller-side kind. Listed as active noise cancellation or ANC against the audio or the ear cups, it is the wearer-side kind. Plenty of headsets list only one because they only have one, so the placement is the tell.

**Q: Is passive noise isolation the same as ANC?**
A: No. Passive isolation is just the physical seal of the ear cushions against your head, blocking sound with no electronics. ANC is an active feature that needs power: the headset senses a steady background through its mics and produces a counter-sound that cancels part of it. A headset can have a good seal and no ANC at all, which is exactly the case with the standard Evolve2 65.
