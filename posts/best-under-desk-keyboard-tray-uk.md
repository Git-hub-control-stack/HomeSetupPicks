# Best Under Desk Keyboard Tray UK 2026

An under-desk keyboard tray does one plain job: it lifts the keyboard and mouse off the desktop onto a platform that slides out below the desk edge. That frees the surface above for a notepad, a coffee or a second screen's base, and it lets you slide the whole board out of the way when you are done. Nearly all of them clamp onto the desk edge with no drilling, so the decisions that actually matter are physical, not clever: how wide the platform is, how far it pulls out, how thick an edge the clamp will grip, and whether the height is fixed or adjustable. This guide puts those four first, and rates three trays you can buy in the UK on the figures their listings actually give, not on how roomy a photo makes them look.

## What decides a keyboard tray

A clamp-on keyboard tray is bought on four physical measurements, in order.

1. **The desk edge the clamp has to grip.** This is the one that stops most trays fitting, so check it first. The clamp needs a flat, accessible front edge with nothing right underneath it. A front apron panel under the lip, or a top thicker than the clamp will open, are the usual blockers. It is the same measuring job as fitting a monitor arm to the front of the desk, and our [monitor arm desk-fit guide](monitor-arm-desk-fit-explained.html) walks through measuring the edge and the no-clear-edge fallback.
2. **Platform size against your keyboard and mouse.** Match the board to your kit: a number-pad keyboard with the mouse riding along too needs the wide trays here (about 69cm across), while a compact keyboard, or a mouse kept up on the desktop, frees you to pick a narrower one.
3. **Pull-out distance.** This sets how far the keyboard comes out from under the desk toward you. More reach lets you push the board fully away when you want the desktop clear; the budget tray here pulls out 21.5cm, the larger VIVO trays more.
4. **Fixed or adjustable height.** Most trays sit at one fixed drop below the desktop, which is fine if the desk is already at a height that suits you. If it is not, the adjustable pick lets you set the keyboard level instead.

Once the tray frees the desktop, the other half of a tidy setup is where the cables run. ## FAQ

**Q: Will a keyboard tray mark or damage my desk?**
A: A tightened clamp bears on the desk edge, so the thing to protect is that edge, not the desktop. The trays here use padded clamp faces that spread the grip, which a solid wood or laminate edge takes without complaint. A thin veneer, a glass top or a painted edge is where you would slip a scrap of felt or card under the clamp first.

**Q: Can I still use a desk drawer with a tray fitted?**
A: Often not, because a sliding tray wants the same space under the front edge that a centre drawer uses. If your desk has a drawer right where the keyboard would sit, plan to mount the tray off to one side of it, or check the tray clears the drawer before you buy.

**Q: Does a keyboard tray work with a standing desk?**
A: Yes. It clamps to the desktop and travels up and down with it, so the keyboard stays in reach at either height. There is nothing extra to set once it is on; it simply rides along with the surface as the desk moves.

**Q: A tray, or just lower the desk?**
A: If your desk height already adjusts, dropping the desk is the simpler move. A tray is the answer when the desk is fixed at a height that suits writing or the monitor but higher than you want the keyboard, since it lowers only the keyboard and mouse and leaves the desktop where it is.
