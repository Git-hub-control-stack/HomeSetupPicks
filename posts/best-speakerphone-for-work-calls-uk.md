## Wired USB or Bluetooth speakerphone? The choice that settles most of it

Before the microphone count or the brand, settle one thing: do you want a cable. At a desk you sit at every day, a wired USB speakerphone is usually the easier life. It is ready the instant your machine wakes, it never drops a connection halfway through a call, and it never picks the worst possible moment to run its battery down. The Speak2 40 and the PowerConf S330 are both wired for exactly that reason. Bluetooth earns its keep when you move: if your calls happen at the desk on Monday and the spare room on Tuesday, or you simply want the desk clear of one more lead, a wireless unit like the Speak2 55 is worth the habit of charging it. If you are honestly at the same desk all week, going cable-free buys a freedom you will never actually use.

The listing titles call the Speak2 40 a portable speakerphone and the Speak2 55 compact and portable. Portable means something different for each, though. The 40 and the PowerConf S330 are wired USB units with no battery in their specs, so wherever they go, a laptop has to go too. ## The microphone count is really a room measurement

Speakerphone listings love a big microphone number, because more sounds like better. What those arrays are really measuring is how large a table they can cover. A six or eight-microphone ring exists to hear everyone seated around a boardroom; on your own, forty to sixty centimetres from the unit, a four-microphone speakerphone hears you clearly and wastes nothing reaching for the far end of a room that is not there.

## Full-duplex and certification: the two specs worth checking

Two things on the spec sheet actually change your calls, and neither is the one printed largest. The first is full-duplex audio. It means both people can talk at the same time and the speakerphone carries both voices instead of ducking one to let the other through. That is the difference between talking and taking turns, and it is where a basic single-microphone puck tends to fall down. The second is platform certification. When a unit is certified for Zoom, Google Meet or Microsoft Teams, its physical mute and answer buttons are tied into the app, so pressing mute on the hardware actually mutes you in the meeting. On an uncertified unit that button might mute the hardware without telling the app, so you sit there muted while the meeting waits, or worse, believe you have muted yourself when the room can still hear every word. Work out which platform you live in and match it: the Speak2 40 is certified for Zoom and Google Meet, the Speak2 55 for Microsoft Teams.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I really need a speakerphone instead of my laptop's built-in mic and speakers?**
A: For the odd quick call, no. But a laptop microphone sits far from your mouth and picks up the fan and the keyboard, and its speakers echo back into that same microphone, which is what makes people ask you to repeat yourself. A speakerphone puts a proper microphone array near you and tunes the speaker so it does not feed back, so you sound clearer and calls feel less like hard work.

**Q: Is a speakerphone or a headset better for work calls?**
A: It depends on where you sit. A speakerphone is hands-free and natural, with nothing to wear over a long day, which suits a private room. A [headset](best-office-headset-for-work-calls-uk.html) or a pair of earbuds keeps the call to you and sounds cleaner to the other side in a noisy or shared space. Plenty of people keep both and choose by the day. Our [headset versus speakerphone explainer](headset-vs-speakerphone-for-work-calls.html) lays out the questions that settle it, and if you go the headset route, the guide to [noise cancelling headphones versus a noise-cancelling mic](headset-anc-vs-noise-cancelling-mic-explained.html) sorts out which feature fixes which problem.

**Q: Can a smart speaker work as a speakerphone for work calls?**
A: Not as well as one built for the job. Many smart speakers will play a call's audio over Bluetooth, but whether they also offer their microphones to your laptop as a call microphone varies from model to model, and they are designed mainly to catch voice commands across a room rather than to carry a two-way conversation. If you want to try yours, check the maker's page says it works as a hands-free call device for a computer; if it does not say so, a speakerphone is the safer buy.

**Q: Will these work with my laptop and my meeting app?**
A: If the laptop has a free USB port, yes, and the Speak2 55 can also join over Bluetooth. The Speak2 40 has both USB-A and USB-C connections, the S330 connects over USB-C or USB-A, and the 55 takes a USB lead as well. To Teams, Zoom or Meet each one is just an audio device, found with no driver or download; pick it as the microphone and speaker in the app's audio settings. The certification covered above only decides whether the mute and call buttons on top also work inside the app.
